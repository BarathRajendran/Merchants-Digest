What 90 Shopify fashion brands show on their product pages

We checked one product page from each of 90 established Shopify apparel, footwear and accessories brands. AI assistants can reach almost all of them. What they find there is thinner than you would expect.

01

Most product pages skip the delivery question

Every buyer wants to know when an order will arrive, whether they can send it back, and whether it will fit. We looked for each of those on the product page itself, not in the footer or on a separate policy page.

Fewer than one in four pages stated a delivery timeframe. Half stated returns terms. Nearly half of the stores stated neither, which leaves the buyer to go looking at exactly the moment they are deciding.

What to doPut an estimated delivery date and a one-line returns summary next to the buy button. It is the cheapest conversion fix in this report.

02

Reviews are shown to people but hidden from machines

Most stores display reviews. Far fewer include the rating in the product's structured data, the machine-readable summary that search engines and AI tools read.

Review apps usually load their widgets with JavaScript after the page arrives. Unless the rating is also written into the page's structured data, a crawler reading the original page can miss it entirely. In our sample, 44% of stores showed reviews that were not in their structured data.

What to doOpen your review app's settings and look for structured data or rich snippets. If it is off, turn it on, then check a product page with Google's Rich Results Test.

03

Structured data covers the basics, not the details

Almost every store had Product structured data with a price, availability and brand. Shopify themes handle that by default.

The details that help a product stand out in shopping results were much rarer. Fewer than four in ten included a GTIN, the product barcode that shopping engines use to match items. Only about one in five described shipping or returns in their structured data, even though Google supports both for product listings.

What to doFill in the barcode field for your products in Shopify, and set up shipping and returns in Google Merchant Center so they appear alongside your listings.

04

The median product page loads code from six other companies

We counted the distinct outside domains each product page loaded scripts from, ignoring Shopify's own. The median was six. A quarter of stores loaded scripts from ten or more.

Email and SMS tools were the most common, followed by review apps. None of these is a problem on its own. The cost is cumulative: every script is something the page has to fetch and run before a buyer can do anything.

From the author

I have built and run several Shopify apps. Almost every app that adds something to the storefront adds a script, and most merchants never remove them when they stop using the app. If you have uninstalled apps, check that their code went with them.

What to doList the apps that put something on your product page. For each one, ask whether it earned its place in the last 90 days. Remove the ones that did not.

05

AI access is not the problem. Content is.

Only 3% of stores blocked any AI crawler in their robots.txt file. And 92% served an llms.txt file, a set of instructions for AI agents. In the files we sampled, most followed the same template generated by Shopify, pointing agents to Shopify's shopping tools, rather than being written by the store.

So the platform has opened the door. What an assistant finds once it gets there is the product page, and as the rest of this report shows, that is where most of the gaps are.

What to doDo not spend time on AI access settings. Spend it on making your product pages say plainly what an assistant would need to recommend you.

Try this week

Five fixes, in order of effort

  • Add an estimated delivery date next to the buy button.
  • Summarise your returns policy in one sentence on every product page.
  • Turn on structured data in your review app and confirm it with Google's Rich Results Test.
  • Fill in product barcodes (GTINs) in Shopify.
  • Remove leftover scripts from apps you no longer use.

How we did it

Established direct-to-consumer apparel, footwear and accessories brands, each verified as running on Shopify. One in-stock product page per store; gift cards, samples and add-ons excluded. We started with 117 candidate brands and checked the 90 we could verify and reach. Fieldwork took place on November 22, 2026.

For each store we fetched one product page and checked the text a buyer sees, the page's structured data, and the outside scripts it loads. We also checked each store's robots.txt file, policy pages and llms.txt file. Five product pages marked as having no delivery timeframe were reopened in a full browser after scripts loaded; none showed one.

Limitations

  • Checks read the HTML a store sends before JavaScript runs. That is also what many AI crawlers read, but widgets added later by scripts can be missed, so text checks are a lower bound.
  • One product page per store. A store may show more on other products.
  • The sample is established brands, which are likely better resourced than the average Shopify store.

Privacy

We do not name the stores we checked. Store-level records are kept private; this report publishes totals only. If you run one of the stores and want to know your own results, use the product page checker.
