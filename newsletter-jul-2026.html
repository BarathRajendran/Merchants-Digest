Discover in AI, buy on your site

Why AI checkout stalled, how rebuilt collections help both buyers and search, and quieter summer updates worth knowing.

AI search & discovery

AI checkout stalled. AI discovery did not.

By mid-summer the pattern was clear. Very few stores had any working in-chat checkout, and most AI assistants were sending buyers to the merchant's own site to pay. Discovery happens in the assistant. The purchase happens on your store.

That means visitors arriving from AI tools usually land mid-decision. They have already compared you with alternatives. Your product page needs to confirm what the assistant said, quickly, rather than starting the pitch from scratch.

Visitors from AI arrive halfway through a decision. Do not make them start again.

Conversion

Collections are a conversion tool

Most stores treat collections as navigation. Buyers treat them as a shortlist. A collection page that groups products the way people actually shop, by use, size or problem, does a lot of selling before anyone opens a product page.

Shopify rebuilt collections this month, so it is a good moment to look at yours again.

Platform

Shopify changes worth knowing

Collections can now be built from several product sources at once and can target specific variants. Handy for 'all blue items' or 'everything in size S' collections without workarounds.

Shopify changelog →

You can now combine more than one product discount on the same item when the products qualify. Useful, but check your margins before you let discounts stack.

Shopify changelog →

US merchants can create UPS return labels for domestic orders straight from the admin, and Shopify Messaging added WhatsApp campaigns.

Try this week

A summer clean-up

  • List your collections and delete or merge any that a new visitor could not tell apart.
  • Rename collections after how people shop, not how your warehouse is organised.
  • If you enable discount stacking, test the worst-case cart before you go live.
